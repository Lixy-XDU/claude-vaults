Maximum Likelihood Line Spectral Estimation in the
Signal Domain: A Rank-Constrained Structured
Matrix Recovery Approach
Xunmeng Wu
, Zai Yang
, Senior Member, IEEE, Petre Stoica
, Life Fellow, IEEE, and Zongben Xu
Abstract—Maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) provides a
well-known benchmark for line spectral estimation and has been
extensively studied in the parameter domain using a variety of
optimization algorithms. To overcome the sensitivity of these al-
gorithms to parameter initialization, in this paper we study the
MLE in the signal domain. We formulate the MLE as an equiv-
alent rank-constrained structured matrix recovery problem that
admits a unique matrix solution containing the signal, from which
the parameters of interest are uniquely retrieved. The alternating
direction method of multipliers (ADMM) is used to solve the rank-
constrained problem and it is shown to have a good convergence
behavior. The proposed approach is generalized to the case of
missing data and arbitrary-dimensional line spectral estimation.
Extensive numerical results are provided that corroborate our
analysis and conﬁrm that the proposed approach globally solves
the MLE problem and outperforms state-of-the-art algorithms.
Index Terms—Line spectral estimation, maximum likelihood
estimation, Hankel-Toeplitz optimization model, rank-constrained
structured matrix recovery, ADMM, nonconvex optimization.

A. Relations to Prior Art
To circumvent the nonconvexity of the MLE, subspace-based
methods such as the multiple signal classiﬁcation (MUSIC) [20]
and the estimation of parameters by rotational invariant tech-
niques (ESPRIT) [21] estimate the frequencies from the signal
subspace of the sample covariance matrix. In the presence of
multiple snapshots (consider for example direction-of-arrival
estimation with a uniform linear array [1]), they are shown to be
large-snapshot realizations of the MLE [22]. They are applicable
to the single snapshot case, considered in this paper, using the
spatial smoothing preprocessing techniques [23], [24], [25], but
only in the full data case.
An approximate maximum likelihood estimator has been
proposed in [26] in the signal domain by relaxing the spectral
sparsity constraint x ∈S0 using a low-rank Hankel method.
The Hankel method has strictly suboptimal performance, hence
an improved double-Hankel (D-Hankel) method was presented
in [27], [28]. But differently from the Hankel-Toeplitz method
proposed in this paper, the D-Hankel method cannot precisely
characterize the set S0. Interestingly, before [26], [27], [28], S0
was precisely characterized via a low-rank positive-semideﬁnite
(PSD) Toeplitz method in [4]. But to the best of our knowledge,
no nonconvex algorithms have been proposed directly based
on this Toeplitz method partly because it does not result in a
uniqueToeplitzsolution,whichcausesconvergenceissuestoany
nonconvex algorithms (see Section II for details). In this paper,
the nonuniqueness issue is completely solved in the proposed
Hankel-Toeplitz method by means of an original combination
of the previous Hankel and Toeplitz methods.
Using the constraint x ∈S0 in (3) we actually control the
spectral sparsity of signal x, an idea that lies at the core of
the so-called sparse methods that have dominated the research
on line spectral estimation of the last two decades; see, e.g.,
the overview article [19]. In sparse methods, one usually swaps
the objective function and the constraint in (3) and solves for
the spectrally sparse signal x by optimizing a sparse metric in a
feasible domain. To overcome the nonconvexity of the problem,
convex relaxation approaches (known also as gridless sparse
methods) have been developed based on the aforementioned
Hankel or Toeplitz models, e.g., atomic norm (or total variation
norm) methods [2], [4], [29], [30], [31], enhanced matrix com-
pletion [28], [32] and covariance ﬁtting [30]. ADMM is also
used to solve the resulting convex problems [29], [30]. These
methods solve the line spectral estimation problem in the signal
domain but do not solve the nonconvex MLE problem.
In the absence of noise, the proposed low-rank Hankel-
Toeplitz method for the MLE is related to a rank minimization
method in [33], where noiseless line spectral estimation was
studied. However, the rank minimization problem in [33] is
based on heuristics and its relation to the spectral sparsity is
unclear. In contrast to this, we show in this paper that the
minimal rank equals the spectral sparsity K if K is small, and the
Hankel-Toeplitz method results in an equivalent reformulation
of the (noisy) MLE and admits a unique solution. The key to
our proof is the classical Carathéodory-Fejér theorem [1], Ch.
4.9.2] and a lemma regarding factorization of Hankel matri-
ces [34]. Using a similar proof, we also extend the proposed
Hankel-Toeplitzmethodfromthe1-Dtohigher-dimensionalline
spectral estimation.

A. A General Algorithmic Framework

VII. CONCLUSION
In this paper, an exact maximum likelihood estimator for
line spectral estimation in the signal domain was proposed
based on rank-constrained structured matrix recovery and the
ADMM algorithm. It is also usable in the missing data and
arbitrary-dimensional cases. It was demonstrated via extensive
numerical results that the proposed approach realizes the exact
MLE and has good performance with respect to convergence,
global optimality and statistical efﬁciency.
The proposed low-rank Hankel-Toeplitz method provides an
exact and unique characterization of the spectral sparsity and
can be used as a general technique in future spectral-sparsity-
based approaches. The current ADMM algorithm needs to
compute a truncated eigen-decomposition in each iteration. A
future research direction will be to develop faster nonconvex
algorithms based on the Hankel-Toeplitz method. Several ref-
erences on nonconvex approaches to low-rank matrix recovery
